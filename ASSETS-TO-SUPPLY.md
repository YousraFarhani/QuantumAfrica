Open the prototype and press **Placeholders** (bottom right). Every slot that needs a real file

---

## A note on the imagery you see now

Every dark figure on the site is drawn in code from Quantum Africa's own subject matter: light
cones, quantum circuits, Bloch spheres, interference fringes, entanglement lattices, absorption
spectra, key-rate curves. The four research cards each carry a figure that matches that project's
actual topic. None of it is stock imagery, none of it is licensed from anyone, and all of it is
yours to keep or replace.

**Why there are no photographs of people yet.** Photographs of real students and researchers found
online are (a) someone's copyright and (b) pictures of identifiable people who never agreed to
appear on your website. Neither is something a scientific organisation should risk. So the two
legitimate routes are your own photographs, or properly licensed ones. Both are below.

---

## 1. Your own photographs — by far the best option

Your webinars, workshops and chapter activities have already happened. Those photos will do more
for credibility than any stock image, because they show real Quantum Africa people.

**Shot list, in priority order**

| # | Shot | Where it goes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Wide group photo — the whole community or a full workshop room | Hero slide 1, About page |
| 2 | Two or three people at a laptop, screen visible, working | Hero slide 2 |
| 3 | A speaker mid-talk, or a full webinar gallery-view screenshot | Hero slide 3, event pages |
| 4 | A chapter team together, ideally with something local in frame | Hero slide 4, chapter pages |
| 5 | Close-up of two people talking or explaining something | Hero slide 5, stories |
| 6 | Someone at a whiteboard with real equations | Research pages |
| 7 | Head-and-shoulders portrait of each leader and chapter lead | People pages |

**Specs:** landscape, 2400×1350 or larger, JPG. Portraits square, 600×600 or larger. Faces sharp
and reasonably lit — a good phone photo beats a bad DSLR one. Send them as they are; I'll crop.

**Consent:** get a short written OK from anyone clearly identifiable, especially students. One line
in an email is enough. Keep them — funders and universities do ask.

## 2. Licensed stock, if you need to launch before the photos exist

Use these only as a bridge, and swap them out as real photos arrive. Free for commercial use with
no attribution required: **Unsplash**, **Pexels**, and **Nappy** (nappy.co specialises in photos of
Black and brown people, which is exactly the gap on the generic sites).

Search terms that actually return usable results:

- `african university students laptop`
- `african students studying together`
- `nigerian university campus students`
- `african woman scientist laboratory`
- `african researcher computer`
- `university lecture hall africa`
- `students workshop laptops`

**Avoid:** anything with visible glowing-blue "AI brain" graphics, anyone in a lab coat holding a
beaker (wrong science), staged handshakes, and any image where the people look like models rather
than students. If a photo would look at home in a bank advert, it will undercut you.
